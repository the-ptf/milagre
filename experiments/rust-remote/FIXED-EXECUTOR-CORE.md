# Private authority-owned fixed executor core

This implements checkpoint 1 of the concluded [fixed-Claim supervisor contract](FIXED-CLAIM-SUPERVISOR-PROPOSAL.md). `PersistentAuthority` owns an inline two-slot executor table. It has no owner queue, automatic dispatcher, asynchronous mailbox output, HTTP adapter, arbitrary executor closure or new public client API. Existing scoped Claim and MemoryHttp behavior remain separate.

## Ownership and internal operations

`policy::persistent::fixed_executor` is crate-private and not wired to a supervisor command. Its entry points are `start_fixed(WorkToken, Outcome, &Clock)`, `poll_fixed(now)`, `observe_fixed(Id)`, `cancel_fixed(Id)` and `discard_fixed(Id, now)`. Outcome selects a fixed 200, 502 or 504 result. Delayed execution, injected panic, admission failure and exit barriers exist only under `cfg(test)`.

The authority table owns the exact Registry permit, canonical device identity, actual JoinHandle, cancellation gate, fixed result, metadata and execution reservations. An Id contains only a random authority-instance identity and checked serial. It owns no worker, Arc, packet or reservation. Dropping or forgetting every observer cannot detach a worker. Neither a worker owner nor its permit escapes; there is no unsafe code, self-referential authority borrow or caller-provided completion/cleanup assertion.

Admission reuses the authoritative Registry inspection and assembly-plus-20,000 ms scheduling bound. It requires a free slot and no existing slot for that device, including across distinct carriers. It reserves resources before allocation/spawn. A newly spawned worker waits on a closed gate; a private construction owner joins on refusal before installation. After final claim validation, the authority installs the exact permit and worker before Begin. Begin checks current authorization, deadline and cancellation under the epoch/gate ordering, records MayHaveRun, then opens the gate. Cancellation that wins before Begin retains NotStarted and zero invocations. Cancellation after Begin never restores NotStarted, even if the thread had not yet executed its fixed result.

A definitive pre-claim resource/spawn/admission refusal invokes nothing and leaves otherwise eligible work unclaimed. Its construction owner cancels and joins any already-spawned thread. A panic found during that join is Worker/dirty authority, not a harmless admission refusal. Injected failure after claim installation aborts and drains; it does not restore Ready or permit replay. This is an internal core API, not the later consuming owner-attempt/Full-return protocol.

## Polling, results and deadlines

Normal polling inspects at most two handles and calls `join()` only after `is_finished()`. Result production, test completion gates and cancellation signals are not join evidence. The private Joined value is constructed only after JoinHandle consumption, and execution credit is released afterward. Observation's `joined` field is diagnostic; no method accepts it as proof. Polling does not wait for an unfinished body or exit barrier. This does not promise hard real-time OS join latency or asynchronous fsync.

The existing authority `tick` calls this poll for installed slots after its Registry maintenance. No new scheduler or completion wake loop is added. Direct private callers must poll or tick; public supervisors cannot submit fixed work yet. Ordinary authority operations and another carrier can progress while a fixed worker waits. The future owner pump still needs independent completion/readiness wakes, fairness and tracked output.

Claim uses one post-spawn time sample for checked +340,000 ms execution and +345,000 ms completion deadlines. Begin samples the clock again after installation; equality refuses with zero invocations and cancels its installed gate. At the first deadline cancellation is requested; only actual exit/join permits a retained 504. If exit consumes the five-second margin, the result expires instead of extending the deadline. Completion equality is TimedOut. Existing 45-second carrier idle expiry, removal, carrier replacement and disconnect may cancel sooner. Removal signals matching workers before its durable record operation; replacement/close reconciliation signals exact closed handles. A stale observer cannot act on a re-paired device or reused slot. Clock regression and poisoned authority/gate state abort; poison recovery is cleanup-only.

Results are `Option<u16>` and stay private in the table. Ready means a joined result is retained, not delivery or Completed work. There is no sealing, output loan or response byte allocation in this core. Explicit `discard_fixed` cancels the exact work, waits through later polling if its thread remains unfinished, and removes the slot only after actual join. Full from discard means cleanup is still pending, not permission to start another execution. It does not reopen the work. Cancelled/expired slots retain bounded metadata and observations until discard or authority teardown. A forgotten observer can therefore consume capacity, but cannot grow memory beyond two slots; this first core has no 512-entry status table or automatic terminal eviction.

## Bounds and terminal cleanup

Each admitted slot reserves a 2 MiB requested thread stack plus 4 KiB execution/bookkeeping allowance and 2 KiB retained metadata from the existing shared connection/device/global budget. The metadata covers the bounded table entry, canonical key clone and signal bookkeeping; results have fixed size. The execution allowance remains charged through actual join. The later 64 KiB output/scratch and tracked mailbox credits are not allocated because this checkpoint cannot emit output. Existing 64 MiB connection/device and 256 MiB global limits remain unchanged. These are service accounting bounds, not a measurement of the OS thread allocator or process RSS. Rust allocator/process abort remains outside typed allocation-failure recovery.

`abort_in_place` signals cancellation without waiting on unfinished workers. Explicit close/retire, `abort_without_commit` and Drop retain ownership through drainage. Core drainage cancels all gates, joins each remaining thread, releases its leases, and records any panic/poison before allowing quiescence. No core gate, epoch or observer lock is held across a join. A preexisting service worker must still be joined separately before terminal persistence. Populating this table inside the supervisor and arranging its retained-slot lock/drain ordering are future integration work; no claim of completed supervisor dispatch is made here.

A panic discovered only during terminal drainage still prevents Clean and returns the existing typed CloseFailure/StoppedAuthority with the original writer guard. Abort stays sticky, including after successful cleanup. Drop never writes Clean. An exit barrier test holds a worker after result production, proves poll returns and other-carrier traffic works, then proves close retains the writer and publishes nothing until actual join. Synchronous terminal IO and unexpectedly unwinding consuming terminal persistence retain the earlier documented limitations. Unsupported fork continuation discards inherited executor ownership before touching inherited synchronization, alongside the existing authority fork guard.

## Immediate Begin refusal safety fix

The independent `rust-remote-fixed-executor-core-review.md` found that installation followed by `begin_fixed(..)?` could return an error without an Id while retaining an unreachable slot. The production `start_fixed` path now handles that error explicitly: cancel the installed gate, retain the entry through actual join, terminalize its exact Registry job, remove the slot, and drop its metadata before returning the failure. A timeout remains TimedOut; other ordinary refusals cancel the work. No claimed request is restored to Ready. This cleanup may synchronously wait for the cancelled fixed worker, as the existing post-spawn refusal path does; there is no detach or force path.

A panic or poison discovered during join, or any unexpected MayHaveRun evidence on the failed-Begin path, preserves sticky Abort and returns Worker. Refund does not permit Clean or prove no effect in those fault cases. No caller supplies a joined/completion flag. Normal refusal cleans only its slot; another active executor and its credits remain owned and unaffected.

Four new tests enter through `start_fixed`, with a one-shot `cfg(test)` intervention immediately before its normal Begin. The original path failed the regression with one remaining slot after returning no Id. Six repeated cancellation failures and six clock advances to exact Begin expiry now each record zero invocations, one actual join, zero orphan slots and exact pre-request budget restoration, followed by successful admission into both slots. Another carrier decrypts output throughout. Further cases cover idle refusal, poison/panic with retained dirty writer ownership, and six failures while the other slot stays active without being cancelled or joined. No deferred-Begin helper is used to trigger these failures.

The remaining review P3s are not closed here: completion status is still poll-cadence-dependent; start/poll/tick must use one time base; canonical-key signalling coverage, standalone visibility/compile-fail evidence, and the later owner status-retention policy remain follow-ups. Module privacy currently prevents worker/permit export. General terminal join remains synchronous. Tracked output and broader integration remain paused for independent review of this fix.

Safety-fix validation: 279 Rust tests, eight doctests, strict all-target Clippy, formatting, example builds and all seven unchanged Node reciprocal/source checks pass.
